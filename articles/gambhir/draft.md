# Gambhir's XI

*Lefties, all-rounders and a long batting order: how one man builds a T20 team, and what happens when he leaves*

Gautam Gambhir has run a T20 dressing room five times now. He captained KKR from 2011 to 2017 and won two titles. He captained Delhi for six games in 2018 before stepping down. He was mentor at Lucknow in 2022 and 2023, which made the playoffs both years, and at KKR in 2024, which won the whole thing. And since July 2024 he has coached India.

Watch enough of those teams and a pattern seems obvious. There's always a left-hander and a right-hander at the crease. There are all-rounders everywhere. The batting goes on forever. I wanted to know if the pattern is real, whether it's his or just the era, and what happened to his teams once he'd gone.

## How I counted

This needed something I didn't have when I wrote about left-right pairs last year: full playing XIs, not just the players who happened to bat or bowl. Hindsight now has every XI from Cricsheet, so I could classify each player by what his team could use him for on the day:

- A **batting option** had batted in the top 7 in at least 40% of his previous 20 T20s, or had 1,500 career T20 runs.
- A **bowling option** had bowled in at least 40% of his previous 20 T20s, or had 150 career T20 overs.
- Both makes an **all-rounder**.

The point is options, so it's based on what he'd been used for in the games leading up to that one, with career numbers as the reputation backstop. It gets the obvious ones right: Kallis, Shakib and Yusuf for KKR in 2012; Bravo, Jadeja and Watson for CSK in 2018; Pollard and both Pandyas for MI in 2019.

For the IPL I've compared every team with the league average in the same seasons, because the impact player rule made every XI twelve players long in 2023.

## The lefties

Let's start with the part everyone knows, because it turns out to be the part everyone has slightly wrong.

![Gambhir at the crease](article/1.png)

The KKR team Gambhir captained to two titles batted with a left-right pair on 55% of its balls, seven points above the league. That sounds like a plan. But it wasn't KKR's left-handers doing it. It was KKR's left-handed captain, opening the batting. **With Gambhir at the crease, 93% of KKR's balls had a left-right pair. Without him, 27%**, a little over half the IPL's rate in those years (47%). His KKR actually picked slightly fewer left-handed batting options than the average team.

Captain Gambhir didn't build a left-handed team. He was the left-hander.

The deliberate version arrives later.

![KKR 2024](article/2.png)

In 2024, with Gambhir as mentor, KKR batted with a left-right pair on **78% of balls**. The IPL managed 55%. Narine opening, Venkatesh Iyer, Rinku Singh: a left-hander was at the crease nearly all the time, and at the other end there was nearly always a right-hander. In 2025 and 2026, after he left, KKR were down to 39%, the lowest of any IPL team in this piece.

And then India.

![India's left-handers](article/4.png)

![India's left-right pairs](article/5.png)

India pick **4.6 left-handed batting options per XI under Gambhir, from 2.7 under Dravid**. No other side picks as many: New Zealand have 3.8, South Africa 2.8, England 1.9, Australia 1.8. India now bat with a left-right pair on 66% of balls, up from 48%.

If you read my left-right piece, you'll remember the conclusion was that a left-right pair works when one of the two can cash in on his matchup, and that simply having one at the crease "isn't all that". Gambhir would like a word.

## The all-rounders

![All-rounders](article/3.png)

This one holds everywhere he's been. Every IPL team Gambhir led or mentored picked more all-rounders than the league did in the same seasons:

- KKR as captain: **+0.5 an XI**
- Delhi in 2018: +0.6 (six games, so a footnote)
- Lucknow as mentor: **+0.9**
- KKR as mentor: +0.5

For context, Dhoni's CSK were +0.35 and Rohit's MI +0.19. And every one of Gambhir's teams went back toward the league once he left: KKR −0.1 from 2018, Lucknow +0.1 from 2024, KKR −0.1 from 2025.

It's not just a selection quirk either. His all-rounders are picked to bat as well as bowl. Under Dravid, India's all-rounders bowled in 89% of their games but made only 16% of the team's runs. Under Gambhir they've bowled in 77% and made 26%. At Lucknow they bowled in only 59%: some of those all-rounders were mostly batting cover. The batting gets longer too. India have 7.7 batting options an XI under Gambhir, from 7.2, and KKR 2024 had 0.6 more than the IPL average.

![India's all-rounders](article/6.png)

Here's the part that surprised me, though. By international standards, Gambhir's India is not especially all-rounder heavy. **India have 2.5 all-rounders an XI; Australia have 4.8**, New Zealand 3.8, England 3.7. What looks like a Gambhir obsession from inside the IPL is closer to normal for the best T20 sides in the world, and India got there late. The thing that's genuinely unusual about his India is the left-handers.

## Did it work?

![With him and without him](article/7.png)

In the IPL, every team won less once he'd gone:

- KKR: 57% of decided games with him as captain, 48% in the six seasons after.
- Lucknow: 59% with him as mentor, 42% since.
- KKR: 79% in 2024, 42% in 2025–26.

Before anyone sends this to a KKR group chat, I should say this is what happened, not why. IPL squads get rebuilt by auctions every few years. The players who made his teams work (Narine, Russell, Shakib, Stoinis) also came and went, and I can't separate the man from the squad with win percentages.

And India? **India win 77% of their decided games under Gambhir. Under Dravid they won 75%.** A completely different team, built a completely different way, winning at almost exactly the same rate. Draw your own conclusions; I've drawn several and I'm keeping them to myself.

## So

Gambhir's teams look the way people say they do, mostly. The all-rounders are real and follow him from team to team. The left-right obsession is real too, but newer than his reputation: as KKR captain he was the left-right pair himself, and it only became a team-building principle at KKR in 2024 and with India. The batting goes deep, though not deeper than England's.

And the teams he leaves behind drift back toward everyone else. Whether that's because he's gone or because the players he picked have gone too is, as a younger me once put it, outside the scope of this piece.
